SolidWorks Assembly & Motion Study

Motor Blower Assembly

A motor blower is a compact device that uses a rotating shaft and an enclosed housing to pull air in and push it out at high speed. These units are commonly found in household appliances, cooling systems, ventilation equipment, and small industrial machines. Their purpose is simple: move air efficiently through a controlled path while protecting the internal rotating parts.

Inside the assembly, the motor spins a shaft connected to the blower ring. As the shaft rotates, air is drawn through the intake and channelled through the curved housing, which guides the airflow in a smooth, controlled direction. The housing is split into upper and lower halves so the internal components can be accessed easily for manufacturing, assembly, or maintenance.

Although this model is simplified, it represents how real blower systems function in everyday products—from vacuum cleaners to cooling fans and ventilation ducts. Building and checking the assembly in SolidWorks helps show how components interact, how airflow paths are shaped, and how rotating parts are supported for smooth operation and durability.

The exploded configuration reveals how the upper and lower housings, blower ring, shaft, bearings and motor body stack together on a common axis while maintaining assembly and service access.

Assembly Layout & Manufacturing Views

Figure 1 – Orthographic drawing & BOM. The drawing sheet combines an exploded view with item balloons, a bill of materials and a full section E–E through the blower and shaft. Detail D defines the mounting plate hole pattern with dimensions suitable for fabrication and inspection.

Figure 2 – Assembled configuration. The completed assembly view highlights the airflow path, motor mounting strategy and bolted joints on the split housing. Materials and appearances are used to clearly distinguish stationary and rotating components.

Figure 3 – Exploded assembly structure. The exploded layout makes the functional grouping clear: upper and lower housing, central blower ring, supported shaft, bearing/coupling interface and motor body. This view was used to verify assembly order, clearances and fastener access.

Motion Study – Shaft Rotation & Blower Alignment

A simple motion study drives the shaft through rotation to confirm the blower ring, housing and motor interface remain clear of interference. This was used as a visual check for mates, alignment and the dynamic envelope of the rotating parts.
